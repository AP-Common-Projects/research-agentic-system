## Summary

This Finance niche report covers 22 discovered channels and 646 total videos. The dataset is dominated by a small group of high-subscriber creators—especially Financial Education, Ben Felix, Tom Crosshill, and ProudMoney—while the majority of channels in the sample operate with fewer than 100,000 subscribers, and many remain under 1,000. Engagement is highly skewed: the largest channels regularly produce six-figure or even seven-figure video views, while micro channels in the long tail rarely exceed a few hundred views.

The branch narratives show that the Finance label is fragmented into distinct sub-communities, including US credit cards, European ETF investing, Indian retirement and tax topics, Canadian tax and brokerage tutorials, UK personal finance, and Malaysian financial planning. This geographic and product-specific fragmentation suggests that a single 'Finance' or even 'Personal Finance' niche label obscures multiple smaller, more coherent audience segments.

Content strategies also diverge sharply. Evidence-based educational channels such as Ben Felix and Tom Crosshill generate high viewership with research-backed long-form videos, while Financial Education uses urgent, emoji-heavy stock-picking commentary that also attracts large but different engagement. Street-interview and salary-transparency formats on Personal Finance with Leila show strong comment activity. Off-topic videos—including fashion hauls, ride-sharing business tutorials, and celebrity billionaire profiles—produce some of the highest outlier scores in the sample.
