1. Expand the Channel Dataset

Expand the dataset from approximately 140 channels to 250–350 Finance YouTube channels.

Do NOT simply add random channels.

Use a structured/stratified sampling strategy so that the dataset represents different Finance categories, channel sizes, countries, formats, and levels of success.

Prioritize:

Large established channels
Medium-sized channels
Small/emerging channels
Recently created channels
Fast-growing channels
Stagnant/low-performing channels

Aim for enough channels within important categories to make comparisons meaningful.

For major categories, aim for at least 5–15 channels per category, preferably more where sufficient channels exist.

3. Fix the Taxonomy

Do NOT use a single field such as:

"Canadian Retirement Planning"

as the complete niche classification.

Separate the dimensions.

For every channel, create independent fields such as:

primary_topic
secondary_topic
geography
target_audience
content_approach
dominant_format

For example:

Primary Topic:
Retirement

Secondary Topic:
Retirement Planning

Geography:
Canada

Audience:
Retirees / 50+

Content Approach:
Educational

Format:
Talking Head

This will allow us to compare the same topic across different countries, audiences and formats.
4. Add Channel Age Information

For every channel collect:

Channel creation date
Approximate age of channel
If possible, date when the channel started producing Finance content

The Finance start date is important because an old channel may have only recently entered Finance.

Do not assume channel creation date equals Finance channel age.

5. Create a New-Channel Cohort

Create a dedicated cohort of approximately 50–100 Finance channels created or substantially started in 2024–2026, where possible.

The goal is to understand what is currently working for new Finance channels.

Prioritize channels that:

Started recently
Have meaningful subscriber growth
Have reached 10K+
Have reached 50K+
Have reached 100K+
Or show unusually strong view velocity despite being small

Also include some recently created channels that remain small/stagnant.

6. Create a "New Winners vs New Losers" Cohort

This is a high-priority analysis dataset.

Find recently created Finance channels and divide them approximately into:

New Winners

Channels started recently that achieved unusually strong growth.

Examples:

100K+ subscribers
Rapidly approaching 100K
High views relative to subscriber count
Strong recent growth
New Losers / Stagnant

Channels started during approximately the same period but that remain relatively small or have weak view performance despite having enough videos.

Try to control for channel age as much as possible.

The objective is to compare:

What are successful new Finance channels doing differently from unsuccessful new Finance channels?

7. Stratify Channels by Size

Record subscriber count and classify channels approximately into:

<10K
10K–50K
50K–100K
100K–500K
500K–1M
1M+

Do not treat all channels as equivalent.

A strategy that works for a 3M-subscriber channel may not be useful for a brand-new channel.

8. Add Growth / Velocity Metrics Where Available

For each channel, collect current:

Subscriber count
Total channel views
Number of videos
Upload frequency

If historical data is available, also collect:

Subscriber growth over 30 days
Subscriber growth over 90 days
Views over 30 days
Views over 90 days

If exact historical subscriber data is unavailable, do NOT fabricate it.

Clearly mark unavailable fields as null/missing.

9. Expand the Video-Level Dataset

For the newly collected channels, collect as many recent long-form videos as practical.

Target approximately:

50–100 recent long-form videos per important channel

For smaller datasets, prioritize at least the most recent 30–50 long-form videos.

Keep Shorts separate.

Do not mix Shorts with long-form analysis.

11. Add Search-vs-Browse Intent Estimate

For every video, estimate the likely traffic/content intent using the title and description.

Use categories such as:

Search-driven
Browse-driven
News-driven
Mixed
Unclear

This is an estimate, not actual YouTube Analytics traffic-source data.

Do NOT claim that this is actual traffic-source data.

The purpose is to distinguish videos such as:

"How to File Form 1099"

from:

"Japan's Money Is Collapsing"

because these likely have very different discovery mechanisms.

Do not invent credentials.

The purpose is to determine whether Finance channels require professional authority or whether strong storytelling can succeed without traditional credentials.

The objective is representative sampling, not forcing equal category sizes.
